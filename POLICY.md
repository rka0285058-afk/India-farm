# Policy Guidelines

How to narrow the gap between agriculture's share of workers (43%) and its share of output (about 16%). Each guideline is tied to a finding of the model in this repository. These are options for discussion, not forecasts, and several involve real trade-offs (see the last section).

## What the model says

| Finding | Implication |
|---|---|
| A non-farm worker produces about 4 times as much as a farm worker (range 3.0 to 4.6) | The gap has two sides: raise output per farm worker, and move workers to higher-productivity jobs. |
| 86% of holdings are under 2 ha and operate 47% of the land; average holding 1.08 ha | Most farms are too small for farming alone to give adequate income. |
| Matching non-farm productivity from land alone would take about 7 ha per household; only about 4% of holdings exceed 4 ha | Consolidating or enlarging farms cannot close the gap on its own. |
| About half of farm household income is wages; 50.2% of households are in debt | Incomes depend on non-farm earnings and credit, not only on yields. |
| A 20% yield loss with falling farm wages cuts marginal-farm income by up to about 20% | Risk protection must cover wage income, not only crops. |
| Moving 5 points of workers out of farming adds about 2 to 7% to GDP (illustrative) | The gain depends on the jobs they move into and what those jobs pay. |

## Guidelines

### 1. Raise output per hectare on the land that exists
- Extend irrigation. It covers 55.8% of gross cropped area, and much less for millets, oilseeds and pulses (Economic Survey 2025-26).
- Correct fertiliser imbalance. The nitrogen, phosphorus and potash ratio has worsened to 10.9:4.1:1 against an ideal of about 4:2:1, according to the Survey.
- Fund research on climate-resilient varieties, and spread precision farming and water-saving practices.
- Target the regions with the largest yield gaps, as the Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana aims to do.

**Indicator to track:** yield per hectare for cereals, pulses and oilseeds against global averages.

### 2. Make small farms viable without forcing land sales
- Legalise and simplify leasing, following the NITI Aayog model law on agricultural land leasing. It lets owners lease without losing ownership, and lets tenants use lease documents to get loans, insurance and relief.
- Strengthen farmer producer organisations so small farmers can share machinery, buy inputs together, and sell at scale.
- Support shared services (custom hiring of machinery, storage, grading) so a 0.4 ha farm can reach scale without owning more land.

**Why:** the model shows farm size alone cannot close the gap. A NABARD working paper estimates adequate cultivation income needs about 1.65 ha, which most holdings do not reach.

**Indicator to track:** share of operated land that is leased, and share of small farmers in producer organisations.

### 3. Protect incomes from climate shocks
- Keep crop insurance affordable and fast. PMFBY has farmer premiums capped at 2% (Kharif) and 1.5% (Rabi) for food grains and oilseeds, a 2026-27 allocation of ₹12,200 crore, and includes tenants and sharecroppers.
- Use satellite yield estimates and land-record checks to cut claim delays and disputes.
- Add protection for wage income in a bad season (for example public works or wage support), since the model shows wage losses drive most of the damage for marginal households.

**Indicator to track:** claim settlement time, tenant enrolment, and income loss in drought years.

### 4. Fix credit and debt
- Widen access to institutional credit for small and tenant farmers, so fewer rely on moneylenders (reported at 30 to 50% interest in the Survey coverage).
- Link credit to lease documents and digital farmer records.

**Indicator to track:** share of indebted households, and the share of loans from institutional sources (NSS 77th round: 50.2% indebted).

### 5. Create non-farm jobs for the workers who leave
- Back labour-intensive manufacturing and exports, since the Survey notes agriculture alone cannot absorb the growing workforce.
- Expand food processing, cold chains and logistics near farming areas, so rural workers can move into higher-paying work without leaving the region.
- Invest in skills. Only about 0.5% of rural people over 15 have formal agricultural training (NSS 77th round), and vocational training is low overall.

**Why:** the model's largest gain comes from moving workers into jobs that pay near the non-farm average. If movers earn only half the average, the gain roughly halves.

**Indicator to track:** farm share of workers (PLFS, now 43.0%), and wages of workers who leave farming.

### 6. Improve data so policy can be targeted
- Keep building farmer IDs and digital crop surveys. About 7.63 crore farmer IDs and 23.5 crore crop plots were reported surveyed by February 2026.
- Refresh land-holding and household-income data. The last full Situation Assessment Survey covers 2018-19, and the last Agriculture Census 2015-16.

**Indicator to track:** share of holdings with a farmer ID and a verified land record.

## Suggested sequencing

| Horizon | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 years | Faster insurance claims, wage protection in bad seasons, farmer IDs and land-record linkage, leasing rules in states without them |
| 2 to 5 years | Irrigation and fertiliser-balance reforms, producer organisation scale-up, rural skills programmes, food processing and cold chain |
| 5 to 10 years | Labour-intensive manufacturing and export growth that absorbs workers leaving farming |

## Trade-offs and open questions

- **Fertiliser pricing:** the Economic Survey suggests raising urea prices to correct the nutrient imbalance. This improves soil use but raises costs for small farmers unless paired with income support.
- **Income support versus price support:** the Survey favours per-acre income support over heavy reliance on minimum support prices. Supporters point to better targeting and fewer market distortions. Critics worry about adequacy for tenants and for crops where price support is the main protection.
- **Leasing:** it can raise efficiency and give tenants access to credit, but needs safeguards so owners do not fear losing land and tenants are not squeezed on rent. State laws differ widely.
- **Job creation:** the reallocation gain is an upper bound. It assumes enough non-farm jobs exist and that farm output does not fall as labour leaves.

## Limits of this analysis

- The model is a national, scenario-based exercise. It does not estimate the effect of any specific scheme.
- Policy options come from official documents and press coverage. Check scheme details against the ministry notifications before relying on them.
- State-level, irrigation and climate data are not yet in the model, so regional recommendations need further work.
